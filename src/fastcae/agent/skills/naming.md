# Finding what the words name
when: the words name something by what it is - the bearing, the hub, the wall, a label like a datum or an axis mark - rather than by id

1. **Ids are names.** A face id names a face; `describe` says which feature it is `in` and what it
   is. Use ids as given, and the feature when the words mean the whole thing.
2. **Several ids for one thing** - "the bearing with" two faces - name one thing by the faces
   given: all of them are it. Something the words then name only by what it is - "and the wall" -
   is none of those faces but something else, to be found: what they face across the open space
   (`relate` `across`).
3. **Named by a face of something else** - "the boss with" a face that is a wall's - means the
   thing of that kind the face touches: `describe` the face for what it touches.
4. **By what it is.** Bearings sit in bores and bosses on the part's main axes: `find` `axis`
   lists the axes largest first, with what is on each. A boss, a bore, a wall near something named:
   `relate` it - `on_axis`, `rises_round`, `stands_on`, `across` - rather than searching the whole
   part.
5. **By a label.** Labels live on the drawing: `search_drawing` for the label as written. A label
   the drawing places on a feature the extraction tied to the model names that feature; one it
   cannot tie is a question.
6. **When more than one fits**: when one is clearly the likelier - it stands where the ribs go, or
   it is the only one of its kind there - write it, and say in an attention line which you took and
   name the other. When none is likelier and the choice would change every design, write what is
   clear and ask, naming the candidates by id with a few words each. Do not keep searching: a few
   questions of the part, then write and ask.
