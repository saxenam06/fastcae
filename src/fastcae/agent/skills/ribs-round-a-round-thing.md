# Ribs all round a round thing
when: ribs round a bearing, a boss, a bore, a hub, an axis named on the drawing

A round thing is a boss (metal inside it) or a bore (metal outside it), and ribs round it are spokes
turning about it, out to what stands round it - standing on the floor it rises from, or on nothing
when open space lies between it and what they reach.

1. **Name it.** An id given: `describe` it - a face belongs to a feature, and `material` says boss
   or bore. A name - the bearing, the hub, a label from the drawing - read the skill on naming.
   For a bore, what shares its axis is often the boss round it: `relate` `on_axis`. Several faces
   given for it - a round one and flat ones - are all of it: the spokes reach out from all of them.
2. **What the spokes reach.** Unnamed: what rises round its floor, read off the part - leave
   `end_on` out. Named ("and the wall"): end on those and on it - `relate` `across` from it finds
   the wall it looks at across the open space.
3. **What they stand on.** When they reach things named, `relate` `between` it and them first: the
   spokes stand on the floor its `stand_on` names, or on nothing when it names none - whatever
   floor the round thing itself rises from. Otherwise, `relate` `stands_on` for it: a floor it
   `rises from`, or for a bore the face it `opens onto` - with every floor `level_with` it, one
   floor the CAD split: stand on them all. `round` says whether together they go all the way round
   it.
4. **Write the block** with `edit_study`: settings `generator` radial, `centre` the round thing,
   `spread` round when the floor goes round it, across when it does not or when the spokes cross
   open space to what they reach.
5. **Look at where the ribs go** in the edit's answer. None, or only a few: say why in an attention
   line - what stopped them, in its words - rather than guessing a fix.

Everything else - count, angle, thickness, radii - is read off the part as ranges. Set only what the
words set.
