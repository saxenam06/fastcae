What this plan does

You already removed the production ribs (housing_baseline.brep) and built designs through the Onshape API. Those ribs came out as plain boxes, with no rounded corners where they meet the walls and bores.

This plan keeps your rib-free housing and changes how ribs are added. The ribs are melted into the housing, so every rib root rounds off by itself. A script checks every design. A fast check on the GPU ranks them all, and an accurate TET10 check in CalculiX confirms the best ones.

None of this needs nTop or Ansys. It runs on free tools on your laptop.

The nTop method we copy

  1. Draw a map of rib lines by projecting a pattern (squares, triangles, hexagons or spokes) onto a wall or around a round barrel.

  2. Turn each line into a rib with a thickness, height, draft and direction. Each of these can change from place to place, for example taller ribs where the strain is high.

  3. Join the ribs to the housing with a blend, which rounds the rib roots automatically.

  4. Trim the rib tops to the height map.

  5. Make hundreds of versions by giving each design its own small settings file.

Five rules

Ribs follow the mould
Ribs stand in the direction the mould opens, or the casting needs extra cores. "Changing orientation" means rotating the rib pattern on the wall, not tilting the ribs. Tilted ribs come only with a later 3D-printed sand-mould mode.
Building is not the same as correct
A blended shape always builds, but it can be quietly wrong. Nothing counts as a design until it passes the checks in step 5.
Videos are not designs
Sweeping one number inside one layout shows real designs. Morphing from one layout to another is for the video only. The in-between shapes can't be cast and are never counted.
Measure what a gearbox housing needs
Bore tilt, vibration frequencies kept away from the gear-mesh frequencies, noise (later), and weight. Peak stress is a safety check on the finalists, not the main score.
The agent suggests, a script checks
The AI agent proposes settings files and code. A script checks every number against the allowed ranges. The agent never invents loads, material values or limits.

How one design moves through the pipeline

First two milestones

M0

Prove the tools

  • Install the Python tools and confirm NVIDIA Warp runs on your RTX 5060.
  • Get CalculiX and gmsh running.
  • Tag the rib-free housing and approve the coloured picture (step 1).
  • Confirm the mould direction for each wall (step 2).
  • Map which ribs you removed, and check the claim that the ring gear gets no ribs.
  • Blend one rib into the housing and look at the rounded root.
  • Solve the original housing, the rib-free housing and 3 designs with both checks, and compare.

Done when all seven are ticked, and the fast check ranks designs the same way as CalculiX, with bending within about 5–10%.

M1

First 64 designs

  • Area: the end wall of the parallel-stage section and its bearing seats.
  • 2 rib patterns. proposed: squares, triangles
  • 4 numbers. proposed: pattern angle on the wall, rib spacing, rib thickness, height scale
  • Rib height from a first strength run: the height map × height scale.
  • 64 designs, each reporting weight, bore tilt and first vibration frequency, plus the margin to the gear-mesh frequencies.
  • Both checks on all 64. This also shows how well the fast check ranks.
  • A sweep video.

Done when there are 64 result rows, a weight-vs-bore-tilt chart and the video.

Steps

  1. Tag the rib-free housing

    Open housing_baseline.brep. A script finds every bore, bolt hole, machined face and mount face from its exact shape and the drawing datums (A, B, D, EV, EW, EX, EY), and colours them in a picture. It also compares the baseline with the original STEP to show where ribs were removed: the baseline has 1,741 surfaces, against 2,149 faces in the original.

    Done when you approve the picture. It's saved as a file that every later step reads.

    scriptyou

  2. Set the mould direction for each wall

    Ribs must stand in the direction the mould opens. The script proposes a direction for each wall from the draft on the existing walls, and you confirm it.

    Done when each wall has one confirmed direction.

    scriptyou

  3. Draw the rib map

    A pattern (squares, triangles, hexagons, spokes) is projected onto a wall or wrapped around a barrel. Each line becomes one rib with a thickness, height, draft and direction. The map also records which rib connects which bearing seat to which mount.

    Done when the same settings file always gives the same rib map.

    scriptagent

  4. Build the shape

    The housing near the wall becomes a distance map, with a fine grid only there. The ribs are blended in so their roots round off, their tops are trimmed to the height map, and the protected areas are cut back exactly. Only the rib area becomes new triangles. It's joined to your exact housing, so everything else stays as your CAD.

    Done when a settings file goes in and a closed STL with rounded rib roots comes out.

    script

  5. Check the shape

    The shape always builds, but it can be quietly wrong. Every design is checked for:

    • ribs too thin for the grid;
    • tiny leftover bits and slivers;
    • blends that accidentally bridge a rib to a nearby boss;
    • protected areas that changed;
    • ribs the mould can't release;
    • very thick spots where cast iron can shrink.

    Done when each design gets pass, warn or reject, with the reason.

    script

  6. Set the loads and run the first strength check

    Bearing forces are worked out from the 325 kNm torque and the gear data (tooth counts, gear sizes, helix and pressure angles), and you sign them off. The mounts use your rubber-mount stiffness once you have it. A first run on the housing gives a strain map, and rib height follows it: taller where the strain is high.

    Done when there's a signed-off loads file and a rib height map.

    scriptyou

  7. Fast check on all designs

    One fixed grid of small bricks covers the housing, and the loads are set once. Each design only changes which bricks are full. The GPU gives weight, bore tilt and the first vibration frequencies quickly. It's good for ranking designs, not for exact stress in a fillet.

    Done when it ranks designs the same way as step 8 (checked in M0 and M1).

    script

  8. Accurate check on the best designs

    The surface is cleaned and filled with 2nd-order tets (TET10) in gmsh, with the middle nodes placed on the true curved surface. CalculiX gives the bore tilt (static) and the vibration frequencies (modal), which are compared with the gear-mesh frequencies. It takes minutes per design: fine for 64, too slow for hundreds, so it runs only on the best ones.

    Done when each design has a results row: weight, tilt at each bearing seat, first frequencies, and margin to the gear-mesh frequencies.

    script

  9. Rebuild the finalists as CAD

    The best 2–3 are rebuilt in CadQuery or Onshape. Fillets on curved cast walls may fail again here, so fix those by hand. The CAD won't exactly match the blended shape, so the accurate check runs again on the real CAD before any decision.

    Done when each finalist has CAD that passes its own check.

    youscript

About the videos: sweeping one number inside one layout shows real designs. Morphing from one layout to another is only for the video. Those in-between shapes can't be cast and never count as designs.

Risks and fixes

"The ring gear gets no ribs" may be wrong
Drawing 254492 is the rear housing and shows a ring-gear face, where a separate ring gear bolts on. The webs there look like part of this casting, and the claim may come from a different model (GB3).Fix: check it in M0 before ruling that area out.
Finalist CAD brings back the fillet problem
Fillets on curved cast walls can fail in CadQuery or Onshape, just as they did in your Onshape runs.Fix: fix the 2–3 finalists by hand, and re-run the accurate check on the real CAD, since it won't exactly match the blended shape.
CalculiX takes minutes per design
That's fine for 64 designs, but slow for hundreds.Fix: the fast GPU check sorts out the bad designs first, and CalculiX runs on the good ones.
The fast check might rank designs wrongly
Blocks make stair-stepped surfaces.Fix: compare it with CalculiX in M0, and run both checks on all 64 designs in M1.
Bearing forces and mount stiffness are missing
Research found only the total torque (325 kNm).Fix: use interim loads from torque plus gear data, report tilt between paired seats, and swap in the real values when they arrive.
Blending can fill holes or touch machined faces
The melt adds material wherever a rib gets close to other surfaces.Fix: protected areas are cut back exactly after every blend, and step 5 confirms they didn't change.
Names we can't judge
The suggestion mentions agentiCAE, Callout, GB3 and ZenryaxAI.Not assessed here. Decide on those separately.

Later, and not at all

Later phases

  • Ribs along load paths, following the direction the force flows
  • Conformal ribs around barrels and on curved box walls
  • Noise: how much the housing surface shakes at the gear-mesh frequencies
  • Thinner housing walls where they carry little load
  • Computer-suggested rib layouts (topology optimization turned into ribs)
  • Automatic search for the best design
  • An AI quick-predict model trained on rib maps and results

Not doing

  • Lattices and gyroids, which can't be cast in iron
  • 3D-printing supports and overhang rules
  • Heat-exchanger structures
